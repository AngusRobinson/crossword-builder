## The American style was a dictionary, not a search

The screening above left `us` immovable: every parameter and an eightfold
increase in time held it between 0.29 and 0.33. That is the shape of a
vocabulary that does not contain the fills, so the next thing to vary was the
vocabulary. UKACD is British, and an American grid checks every cell, which
puts the weight on the short entries -- 1,245 three-letter entries against
Spread the Wordlist's 4,308, and 5,054 fifteen-letter against 29,435.

Held-out lists, today's defaults, one dictionary swapped, 36 matched pairs.
Generated by `experiment/dictionary.py`, read by `experiment/analyse_dictionary.py`.

| style | American fills | cover | UKACD fills | cover |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| british | 12/12 100% | 0.713 | 12/12 100% | 0.693 |
| barred | 12/12 100% | **0.564** | 12/12 100% | 0.406 |
| us | **10/12 83%** | **0.733** | 6/12 50% | 0.599 |

Four American lists go from failing to filling and none go the other way.
Note the UKACD baseline here is 50%, not the 32% above: `relax` at 10 had
already taken it there, and the dictionary is a second, separate lift on top.

Barred gains as much in coverage as American does, which was not the
prediction -- a barred grid is dense in short entries too, and that is where
the American list is fat. Whether the words it seats *belong* in a Mephisto is
a separate question from whether they fit, and this table cannot answer it.

Two limits worth keeping in view. Spread the Wordlist stops at fifteen letters,
because a 15x15 is the American grid, so it cannot fill a 21x21 jumbo at all --
the runner records that pair as unserved rather than failing it. And the list
is entirely lowercase with its phrases pre-collapsed, so `--proper` and
`--allow-phrases` have nothing to filter on.
